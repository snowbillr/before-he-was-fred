## Factual corrections & uncertainties (plan docs vs. the letter)

1. **Chronology (now fixed in `route.md` and the chapter docs; the German dateline is 25 Dezember 1939).** The letter says: woke in the lonely house near Taşlıçay on Dec. 25 (Letter p. 6); left Taşlıçay on Dec. 27 and reached Karagossa at dark the same day (Letter p. 7); left Karagossa the morning of Dec. 31 for Alaskirt (Letter p. 8); New Year's Eve in Eleşkirt; Jan. 1 to Türkali and the failed pass attempt (Letter p. 8–9); Jan. 6 the successful crossing to Tahir and Delal (Letter p. 10). `route.md` puts Taşlıçay on Dec. 28 and Karaköse on Dec. 29 — those dates don't match the letter. By the same logic, Bazargan/Gürbulak/Bayazit/Kızıldize fall around Dec. 23–24 (Letter p. 4–5), so the chapter 2/3 boundary date "Dec. 27" in the brief is also probably early by ~3–4 days. Recommend rebuilding dates from the letter.
2. **The Erzincan earthquake (01:57 local, Dec. 27, 1939) happened while Fritz was at/near Taşlıçay**, ~300 km east of the epicentral area — the letter never mentions feeling it; Fritz first refers to it in hindsight while recounting the Taşlıçay delay (Letter p. 7), and the rail damage comes up from Hasankale and Erzurum onward (pp. 11–12). Worth a "meanwhile…" card. Source: [1939 Erzincan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_Erzincan_earthquake).
3. **"Karaköse in 1939" (`additional_info.md`) is imprecise.** The town's official name was **Karakilise** until 1946, later Karaköse, and today Ağrı. Fritz's "Karagossa (Karagöse)" is his rendering of the colloquial Karaköse. Source: [Ağrı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C4%9Fr%C4%B1).
4. **Eleşkirt naming.** In 1939 the district seat was the village of Zedikan (renamed Eleşkirt); the historic Alashkert/Toprakkale is a different site. Fritz's "Alaskirt" = modern Eleşkirt town. Source: [Eleşkirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ele%C5%9Fkirt).
5. **Locations to treat as approximate:** "Kızıldizce/Kızıldice" — OpenStreetMap/Nominatim resolves "Kızıldize" to the area of Ortadirek, Doğubayazıt (39.501, 43.991), but the letter puts Kızıldice ~25 km *beyond* Bayazit toward Taşlıçay (Letter p. 5), so the route.md coordinate is plausible and the identification is unconfirmed. "Türkali" has no reliable gazetteer match; the village of **Tahir** (Eleşkirt district) is at 39.864, 42.438 (OSM). "Delal" (20 km beyond Tahir, 16 km before Horasan, Letter p. 10–11) was not found under that name; one *speculative* candidate is Delibaba (39.988, 42.306). Flag the map pins as approximate.
6. **Pass elevation:** any specific elevation in the plan docs for Tahir Pass (Tahir Geçidi) could not be verified from a free authoritative source in this research — cite one before publishing or drop the number.
7. **Name collision:** "Taserkand" (chapter 1, Iran) and "Taşlıçay" (this chapter) are different places; don't merge them.

Mount Ararat (Ağrı Dağı, 5,137 m) stands just inside Turkey near the 1939 Iran and Soviet borders; it was the backdrop for the whole drive from Bazargan to Taşlıçay (Letter p. 4–5). Sources: [Mount Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat).

### Context card — "66" (Sechsundsechzig)
Sechsundsechzig ("66") is a fast point-trick game of the "marriage" type for 2–4 players with 24 cards (A, 10, K, Q, J, 9), popular in German-speaking Central Europe; the aim is to reach 66 card points first, with king–queen "marriages" scoring bonuses. It was a natural way for German-speaking travellers to pass snowbound hours (Letter p. 5; again on the train, Letter p. 14). Source: [Sixty-six (card game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixty-six_(card_game)).

### Context card — Ford and Chevrolet trucks in 1930s Turkey
American light trucks dominated motor transport in interwar Turkey and Iran; in 1929 the Turkish parliament granted Ford the right to build an assembly plant in a free zone at Tophane, Istanbul. On eastern Anatolian roads they served as freight carriers and informal buses, with passengers riding on top of the load. Fritz's account of both trucks bogging down and running dry (Letter p. 5–6) shows how marginal winter road travel was before the railway reached the east. Source: [Ford Motor Company in Turkey — Ford Otosan history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Otosan).

### Context card — "Meanwhile, in Erzincan" (night of Dec. 26–27)
At 01:57 local time on Dec. 27, 1939 a magnitude 7.8 earthquake on the North Anatolian Fault destroyed Erzincan and much of the surrounding region — 32,968 people died. Fritz, then at Taşlıçay (Letter p. 6–7), would not learn of it until Erzurum (Letter p. 12), and would ride through the ruins a few days later (Letter p. 14). Source: [1939 Erzincan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_Erzincan_earthquake).

### Context card — "Puff" and tavla
"Puff" is the old German name for backgammon-type games; in Turkey the game is **tavla**, and its dice calls are traditionally given in Persian-derived numbers (yek, dü, se, cihar, penç, şeş). Playing tavla through the days of waiting in Karakilise (Letter p. 7) was exactly what locals did in winter coffeehouses. Sources: [Tables (board game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tables_(board_game)), [Backgammon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backgammon).

### Context card — Karakilise, a frontier province capital
The town was officially Karakilise until 1946, became Karaköse, and is now Ağrı. In the 1930s it was a small garrison and administrative town on the main Iran–Erzurum road. Source: [Ağrı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C4%9Fr%C4%B1).

Eleşkirt became the district seat when the administration moved from Toprakkale to the village of Zedikan in the early Republic, and took the old name Alashkert/Eleşkirt. Fritz's New Year's Eve here (Letter p. 8) fell just days after the Erzincan disaster and four months into WWII. Source: [Eleşkirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ele%C5%9Fkirt).

### Context card — the Jandarma
The Turkish Gendarmerie (Jandarma) policed rural Turkey, including roads and mountain passes in the east; travellers in trouble were routinely taken in at gendarmerie posts. In Fritz's letter the gendarmes appear as both protectors and hosts (Letter p. 9–10). Source: [Gendarmerie General Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie_General_Command).

### Context card — the Tahir Pass
The Tahir Pass (Tahir Geçidi) carries the Ağrı–Erzurum road over the mountains between the Eleşkirt plain and the Aras basin near Horasan; the Tahir township sits where Ağrı (Eleşkirt), Erzurum (Horasan) and Kars (Sarıkamış) provinces meet. Fritz's party failed on Jan. 1 and crossed only on Jan. 6 (Letter p. 8–10). Source: [Tahir, Eleşkirt (tr)](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahir,_Ele%C5%9Fkirt). (Elevation figures unverified.)

### Context card — tea, flatbread and honey
Black tea from a samovar or çaydanlık, flatbread (lavaş/yufka) and honey were standard hospitality in eastern Anatolian households; the Erzurum–Ağrı highlands are known for honey. The road-keeper's breakfast (Letter p. 10) is one of the warmest moments in the letter. Source: [Turkish tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_tea), [Lavash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavash).
