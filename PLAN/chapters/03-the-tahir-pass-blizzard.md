# Chapter 3: The Tahir Pass Blizzard
**Route:** Kızıldize → Taşlıçay → Karaköse (Ağrı) → Eleşkirt → Türkali → Tahir Pass → Tahir → Delal
**Dates:** Dec 23, 1939 – Jan 6, 1940
**Mode of travel:** Windowless Ford truck, horse-drawn sleds, and on foot

> **Dates:** The letter's German dateline reads "25. Dezember 1939, Taslicay" (the English translation had "29"), and Fritz writes that the farewell in Tehran was "heute vor 6 Tagen" (6 days ago). So they left Tehran on Dec 19, reached Bazargan on Dec 23 (matching the telegram "vom 23."), and woke in Taşlıçay on Dec 25. The Dec 25 and Dec 27 dates on letter pp. 6–7 are correct.

## Chapter Overview
This is the emotional core of the letter. A night stranded in the snow at −35°C, an axle failure, an overturned truck, days of waiting in Karaköse, and New Year's Eve with only tea. Then a failed sled crossing of the Tahir Pass in a blizzard, five days trapped in a vermin-ridden hut in Türkali, and the successful crossing on January 6. Most of this chapter happens *between* towns, so the legs matter more than the stops.

## Stop: Kızıldize
**Coordinates:** ~39.6300° N, 43.8300° E (approximate) | **Date:** Dec 23–24, 1939

### Narrative
"Unwashed, frozen, and hungry" in a dark, bitterly cold inn, they waited because the chauffeurs saw no way through the snow [Letter p. 5 — "Unwashed, frozen, and hungry"]. They played the card game 66 "to hundred-thousand" and then started over [Letter p. 5 — "played 66 up to a hundred thousand"]. Hunger drove them to eat unappetizing chunks of meat with "even more unappetizing egg" over them, which they "pounced on like wolves" [Letter p. 5 — "pounced on it like wolves"]. Their chauffeur had to go back to Gürbulak about the confiscated tires, so they were transferred to another truck [Letter p. 5 — "go back to Gürbulak because of the tires"].

## Leg: Kızıldize → Taşlıçay (night drive in the snow)
**Date:** Dec 24–25, 1939 (late afternoon through the night)

### Narrative
The replacement was a Ford with no side windows, "only sketchily covered with a tarpaulin". A Chevrolet followed with Strauss's large luggage [Letter p. 5 — "a Ford that had no side windows"]. Only two snowed-over ruts marked the road. After 20 km the Ford stuck, and from then on the men shoveled and pushed again and again, sinking above the knee. Fritz's nose froze shut, and he "would have gladly given a kingdom for a warm bed" [Letter p. 5 — "given a kingdom for a warm bed"]. Then the Chevrolet ran out of gasoline [Letter p. 6 — "The gasoline was finished"]. Half asleep, Fritz felt the Ford rolling backward. Dogs barked at a lonely house 30 m from the road, where a stranger gave them the better of his two beds [Letter p. 6 — "a lonely house"].

## Stop: Taşlıçay
**Coordinates:** 39.6328° N, 43.3556° E | **Date:** Dec 25–27, 1939

### Narrative
They woke to a brilliant sunny winter day. In the village elder's house they talked with their hands over tea. In the inn they learned that the night had brought "35 degrees of cold" and that they had to wait for gasoline from Karaköse, 35 km away [Letter p. 6 — "35 degrees below zero"]. Fritz learned the Turkish numbers, drew up a word list, and began this very letter. He told his father to trace the Euphrates north from Baghdad on the map to find the place [Letter p. 6 — "search in vain with your finger on the map"]. Looking back, Fritz realized the delay may have saved their lives: without it they would have arrived "right into the middle of the earthquake" [Letter p. 7 — "right into the middle of the earthquake"].

### Features & Media
* **Primary Source Highlight:** The opening dateline of the letter, "Taslicay (Asia Minor), 25 December 1939" [Letter p. 1 — "Taslicay"].
* **Map Inset:** Fritz's directions, traced along the Euphrates from Baghdad to Taşlıçay.
* **Context Card — "Meanwhile":** The Erzincan earthquake struck at about 2 AM on Dec 27, the morning they left Taşlıçay, roughly 300 km to the west. The letter does not mention it until Erzurum (see Chapter 4).

## Leg: Taşlıçay → Karaköse (broken axle, overturned truck, first sled)
**Date:** Dec 27, 1939

### Narrative
The gasoline came, but in the haze the Ford drove into the Chevrolet's ruts and broke an axle straining to get free [Letter p. 7 — "an axle broke"]. They rented a sled in the village. Fritz's feet froze in his mountain boots, and Strauss had his invaluable ski cap but two left gloves [Letter p. 7 — "2 left gloves"]. A few kilometers on, they found the Chevrolet overturned off the road with Strauss's suitcases scattered in the snow [Letter p. 7 — "overturned"]. They reached Karaköse at dark.

## Stop: Karaköse (today Ağrı)
**Coordinates:** 39.7194° N, 43.0503° E | **Date:** Dec 27–31, 1939

### Narrative
The road was "closed for cars for an indefinite time". Days passed with inquiries at the bus office, cards and backgammon, and writing. The Persian-speaking hotel keepers were their only interpreters [Letter p. 7 — "closed for cars for an indefinite time"]. The only way out was a 185 km sled ride to Erzurum. They made the chauffeur pay half of it, 9 Lira, because they had already paid through to Trabzon [Letter p. 8 — "9 Lira"].

### Features & Media
* **Context Card:** Karaköse was later renamed Ağrı (year to verify) after the province and Mount Ararat (*Ağrı Dağı*).

## Leg: Karaköse → Eleşkirt (first day by horse sled)
**Date:** Dec 31, 1939

### Narrative
The horses "did not look particularly trustworthy". The driver stopped for a chat with everyone they met and then ran behind, so the passengers walked much of the way [Letter p. 8 — "did not look particularly trustworthy"].

## Stop: Eleşkirt ("Alaskirt")
**Coordinates:** 39.7944° N, 42.6739° E | **Date:** Dec 31, 1939 – Jan 1, 1940 (New Year's Eve)

### Narrative
After 35 km they ate roast mutton legs, "the only edible thing", and spent New Year's Eve drinking tea. Fritz thought of family in Shanghai and toasted Aunt Lottchen with tea [Letter p. 8 — "thought of earlier New Year's Eves"]. At 5 AM a faint "Prosit Neujahr, Fritz" woke him. Strauss had barely slept because water dripped onto his bed all night [Letter p. 8 — "Prosit Neujahr"].

### Features & Media
* **Journal Highlight:** New Year's Eve 1939, written up as a quiet vignette.

## Leg: Eleşkirt → Türkali → Tahir Pass (the failed crossing)
**Date:** Jan 1, 1940

### Narrative
Three sleds set out in drizzling rain, joined by a young Turk with his wife and four children. At Türkali's gendarmerie the driver telephoned Tahir, 18 km ahead, and was told the road was free [Letter p. 8 — "telephoned to Tahir"]. Within a few hundred meters the track vanished. The men walked ahead into a storm of ice crystals. Near the ridge the wind knocked Fritz flat, and visibility fell below 5 m [Letter p. 8 — "The dreadful storm had blown me over"]. Strauss disappeared for over an hour. He came back having sunk "up to his neck", lost a rubber shoe, and crawled back on his belly [Letter p. 8 — "sunk up to his neck"]. A horse fell and rolled 30 m down the slope. The father of the family fired a revolver twice into the air, and "the bang died in the storm" [Letter p. 9 — "shot twice into the air"]. After more than two hours they turned back and left two luggage sleds on the mountain [Letter p. 9 — "over 2 hours in this hell"].

## Stop: Türkali (stranded)
**Coordinates:** ~39.8300° N, 42.4500° E (approximate) | **Date:** Jan 1–6, 1940

### Narrative
The gendarmes scraped off their ice crusts and gave them coffee, tea and bread [Letter p. 9 — "let our ice crusts be scraped off"]. Then came three nights in "a dark hole" of stones and mud, lit only by a 20 cm skylight drifted over with snow [Letter p. 6 — "here in a dark hole"]. There were no cigarettes and no tea, they drank melted snow from a shared tin mug, and they were "almost eaten up by vermin" [Letter p. 9 — "almost eaten up by vermin"]. A fellow traveler served the singed head of a slaughtered kid goat. A gramophone astonished villagers who had never seen one [Letter p. 9 — "gramophone"]. On Jan 4 the luggage sleds were recovered, and the gendarmes took them in [Letter p. 9 — "brought the sleds with the luggage back"]. On Jan 5 at 3 PM the first sled came over from the other side, "as a shipwrecked person on a deserted island must feel" [Letter p. 9 — "shipwrecked person"].

### Features & Media
* **Primary Source Highlight:** The opening of the Türkali entry, "How immensely depressing this decade starts for us" [Letter p. 6 — "How endlessly depressing this decade is starting"].

## Leg: Türkali → Tahir Pass → Tahir → Delal (the crossing)
**Date:** Jan 6, 1940, 6:00 AM → 8:30 PM

### Narrative
Fritz was awake from 1 AM listening for wind. At 4:30 he watched the stars fade and went to wake the drivers [Letter p. 10 — "saw the stars fade"]. The luggage rode while they walked behind in clear, warm sunshine. They passed the scene of their disaster, crossed the pass height after two hours, and walked two more hours along the ridge [Letter p. 10 — "passed after 2 hours the pass height"]. Going downhill they glided on the luggage. Fritz was tipped into the snow under the sled and left behind 50 m while Strauss told a story [Letter p. 10 — "the sled on top of me"]. In Tahir by 10 AM, villagers crowded the "Doctor" with ailments, and Strauss produced only a pocketknife [Letter p. 10 — "pulled out then awkwardly his pocketknife"]. Road workers served tea, flatbread and honey and asked them to sign a guestbook. Strauss gave them his multi-tool [Letter p. 10 — "tea, flatbread, and honey"]. Then came 20 km by sled downhill to Delal.

## Stop: Delal
**Coordinates:** ~39.8500° N, 42.2500° E (approximate) | **Date:** Jan 6, 1940, 8:30 PM

### Narrative
"We are saved! Allah and his colleagues from the other religions be thanked!" Delal had only four houses, but one was an inn, and "the road to Erzurum is free" [Letter p. 9 — "We are saved!"].

### Features & Media
* **Visual Transition:** The frost overlay melts away.
