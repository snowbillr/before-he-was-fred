# Chapter 5: Across the Aegean
**Route:** Istanbul → (Orient Express) → Pythion → Alexandroupolis → Salonika → Thebes → Piraeus → (*Egitto*) → Corinth Canal → Brindisi → Foggia → Naples
**Dates:** Jan 16–24, 1940
**Mode of travel:** Ferry, tram, taxis, the Orient Express under police escort, a diesel railcar, Greek trains, the Italian motor ship *Egitto* (the letter calls it a "luxury steamer"), a bus, and an overnight train

## Chapter Overview
The calm of the Marmara gives way to a bureaucratic ordeal. Istanbul police charge them with overstaying, a court sentences them (the sentence is remitted), they are expelled the same day, and they miss the ship meant to carry them to their transatlantic liner. What follows is a frantic race across Thrace, Macedonia and the Ionian Sea, train to railcar to train to ship to bus. The suspense is whether they will reach Naples before the *Conte di Savoia* sails.

**Date:** Jan 16, 1940, 7:00 AM

### Narrative
The ferry was barely 20 m from the station. "Over there in the rain lay Europe." ## Stop: Istanbul
**Coordinates:** 41.0082° N, 28.9784° E | **Date:** Jan 16–19, 1940

### Narrative
Fritz limped to a Turkish bath in yellow rubber galoshes over his thick Tabriz socks [Letter p. 15 — "thick Tabriz socks"](../letter/pages/page-15.json#p15-s29). Mr. Goldenberg, the brother of a family friend, helped them with money and arranged a passage on the Italian ship *Città di Bari* to connect with the *Conte di Savoia* in Naples [Letter p. 15 — "Mr. Goldenberg"](../letter/pages/page-15.json#p15-s30). At the university clinic a deep abscess on Fritz's heel was opened [Letter p. 15 — "a deep-seated abscess was opened"](../letter/pages/page-15.json#p15-s35). Then the police charged them with overstaying the 24-hour registration and 14-day limits [Letter p. 16 — "registration period"](../letter/pages/page-16.json#p16-s01). Days of tram rides, courts and lunch breaks followed. A judge sentenced them to 14 days' prison and a 25-Lira fine, remitted "because it was the first time" [Letter p. 16 — "14 days in prison and a fine of 25 lira"](../letter/pages/page-16.json#p16-s37). The *Città di Bari* sailed without them. A fingerprint card for another Fritz, son of another Wilhelm, briefly implicated Fritz, until photos showed "the other guy was bald" [Letter p. 17 — "the other man was bald"](../letter/pages/page-17.json#p17-s07). By 5:30 PM on Jan 19 they had exit visas and a deportation order to leave that same day [Letter p. 17 — "the expulsion order"](../letter/pages/page-17.json#p17-s08).

### Features & Media
* **Document Card:** A 1940 Turkish deportation order and exit visa (generic archival example, if one can be found).
* **Primary Source Highlight:** Fritz's outburst to the commissar: "we had no fault in the snowstorms and earthquakes" [Letter p. 16 — "the snowstorms and earthquakes"](../letter/pages/page-16.json#p16-s16).

## Leg: Istanbul → Pythion (Orient Express under police escort)
**Date:** Jan 19–20, 1940, 10:30 PM

### Narrative
Railway police held them for four hours without letting them phone Goldenberg [Letter p. 17 — "railway police, where they kept us"](../letter/pages/page-17.json#p17-s13). Then they rode in their own Orient Express compartment with a policeman, under a poster reading "Istanbul welcomes you" [Letter p. 17 — "Istanbul welcomes you"](../letter/pages/page-17.json#p17-s21).

## Stop: Pythion (Greek border)
**Coordinates:** 41.3783° N, 26.6119° E | **Date:** Jan 20, 1940

### Narrative
The express was late and the Athens connection had gone. The next train was 24 hours away, which seemed to end their hopes of the *Città di Bari* and the *Conte di Savoia* [Letter p. 17 — "connecting train to Athens was gone"](../letter/pages/page-17.json#p17-s24). They paid for customs seals on their trunks and spent the night in the waiting room of "that tiny hamlet".

## Leg: Pythion → Alexandroupolis → Salonika (diesel railcar and Macedonian train)
**Date:** Jan 21, 1940

### Narrative
A diesel railcar crossed Thrace along a flooded river plain full of cranes [Letter p. 17 — "diesel railcar"](../letter/pages/page-17.json#p17-s27). After Alexandroupolis came window seats in an express-train compartment through green Macedonian hills with snow peaks behind. There was no tobacco in sight, and none of the famous sun [Letter p. 17 — "through Macedonia was beautiful"](../letter/pages/page-17.json#p17-s30). They reached Salonika at 10 PM and spent a cramped night in a full compartment.

### Features & Media
* **Nature Card:** Cranes and wetlands of the Evros (Maritsa) delta.

**Date:** Jan 22, 1940

### Narrative
Past Thebes the line descended toward Athens, with "the truly blue Mediterranean Sea" behind. They went straight on to Piraeus, arriving at 11:30 [Letter p. 17 — "Shortly past Thebes"](../letter/pages/page-17.json#p17-s35). *(Letter pages 18–19 are missing here.)*

## Stop: Piraeus
**Coordinates:** 37.9475° N, 23.6372° E | **Date:** Jan 22, 1940, noon

### Narrative
A ship for Brindisi was leaving at 12. They rushed by an "antediluvian cab" and a motorboat paid in US dollars and reached the 3,500-ton *Egitto* "at the last second" [Letter p. 20 — "reached the ship at the last second"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s05).

## Leg: Piraeus → Corinth Canal → Brindisi (*Egitto*)
**Date:** Jan 22–23, 1940

### Narrative
In the late afternoon a small tug towed the ship through the Corinth Canal (Fritz says 3 km; it is about 6.4 km), "a marvel of engineering" [Letter p. 20 — "towed by a small tug"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s10). Hope returned: the *Conte di Savoia* would sail on the 24th [Letter p. 20 — "the Conte di Savoia was to sail from Naples"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s13).

### Features & Media
* **Visual Highlight:** The canal's sheer 80 m limestone walls, with an archival photo comparison.

## Stop: Brindisi
**Coordinates:** 40.6327° N, 17.9419° E | **Date:** Jan 23, 1940

### Narrative
Passport and baggage checks ran so long that the 1 PM Naples train left while the baggage clerk was at breakfast. Strauss was on board; Fritz was left behind with the trunks. "So there I sat, alone in Brindisi" [Letter p. 20 — "alone in Brindisi"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s17).

## Leg: Brindisi → Foggia → Naples (trains, alone)
**Date:** Jan 23–24, 1940

### Narrative
Fritz took a 3 PM train to Foggia hoping to beat Strauss, who was on the southern route. It reached Foggia not shortly after 5 but only around 1 (the hour is hard to read) [Letter p. 20 — "the train then arrived in Foggia"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s18). Luckily another train ran after midnight, and he reached Naples at 6:30 AM to find Strauss already waiting [Letter p. 20 — "landed in Naples at half past 6 in the morning"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s19).

### Features & Media
* **Split Map:** Two routes animated in parallel, Strauss by the southern route and Fritz by train via Foggia.

**Coordinates:** 40.8518° N, 14.2681° E | **Date:** Jan 24, 1940, morning

### Narrative
Fritz went to the main post office to telephone Berlin, but the official refused the call (part of this passage is too smudged to read) [Letter p. 20 — "to telephone Berlin"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s21).
