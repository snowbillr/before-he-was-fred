# Chapter 5: Across the Aegean
**Route:** Istanbul → (Orient Express) → Pythion → Alexandroupolis → Salonika → Thebes → Piraeus → (SS *Egitto*) → Corinth Canal → Brindisi → Foggia → Naples
**Dates:** Jan 16–24, 1940
**Mode of travel:** Ferry, tram, taxis, the Orient Express under police escort, a diesel railcar, Greek trains, the Italian steamer *Egitto*, a bus, and an overnight train

## Chapter Overview
The calm of the Marmara gives way to a bureaucratic ordeal. Istanbul police charge them with overstaying, a court sentences them (the sentence is remitted), they are expelled the same day, and they miss the ship meant to carry them to their transatlantic liner. What follows is a frantic race across Thrace, Macedonia and the Ionian Sea, train to railcar to train to ship to bus. The suspense is whether they will reach Naples before the *Conte di Savoia* sails.

### Narrative
The ferry was barely 20 m from the station. "Over there in the rain lay Europe." They took leave of the continent that had hosted them for almost three years and landed in Pera after 20 minutes [Letter p. 15 — "Over there in the rain lay Europe"].

## Stop: Istanbul
**Coordinates:** 41.0082° N, 28.9784° E | **Date:** Jan 16–19, 1940

### Narrative
Fritz limped to a Turkish bath in yellow rubber galoshes over his thick Tabriz socks [Letter p. 15 — "yellow rubber galoshes over the thick Tabriz socks"]. Mr. Goldenberg, the brother of a family friend, helped them with money and arranged a passage on the Italian ship *Città di Bari* to connect with the *Conte di Savoia* in Naples [Letter p. 15 — "Mr. Goldenberg"]. At the university clinic a deep abscess on Fritz's heel was opened [Letter p. 15 — "a deep-seated abscess was opened"]. Then the police charged them with overstaying the 24-hour registration and 14-day limits [Letter p. 16 — "exceeded the registration period of 24 hours"]. Days of tram rides, courts and lunch breaks followed. A judge sentenced them to 14 days' prison and a 25-Lira fine, remitted "because it was the first time" [Letter p. 16 — "14 days prison and 25 Lira fine"]. The *Città di Bari* sailed without them. A fingerprint card for another Fritz, son of another Wilhelm, briefly implicated Fritz, until photos showed "the other guy was bald" [Letter p. 17 — "the other guy was bald"]. By 5:30 PM on Jan 19 they had exit visas and a deportation order to leave that same day [Letter p. 17 — "deportation order"].

### Features & Media
* **Document Card:** A 1940 Turkish deportation order and exit visa (generic archival example, if one can be found).
* **Primary Source Highlight:** Fritz's outburst to the commissar: "we had no fault in the snowstorms and earthquakes" [Letter p. 16 — "no fault in the snowstorms and earthquakes"].

## Leg: Istanbul → Pythion (Orient Express under police escort)
**Date:** Jan 19–20, 1940, 10:30 PM

### Narrative
Railway police held them for four hours without letting them phone Goldenberg [Letter p. 17 — "railway police, where they kept us"]. Then they rode in their own Orient Express compartment with a policeman, under a poster reading "Istanbul welcomes you" [Letter p. 17 — "Istanbul welcomes you"].

### Features & Media
* **Archival Photography:** The Simplon-Orient Express at Sirkeci station.

## Stop: Pythion (Greek border)
**Coordinates:** 41.3783° N, 26.6119° E | **Date:** Jan 20, 1940

### Narrative
The express was late and the Athens connection had gone. The next train was 24 hours away, which seemed to end their hopes of the *Città di Bari* and the *Conte di Savoia* [Letter p. 17 — "connecting train to Athens was gone"]. They paid for customs seals on their trunks and spent the night in the waiting room of "that tiny hamlet".

## Leg: Pythion → Alexandroupolis → Salonika (diesel railcar and Macedonian train)
**Date:** Jan 21, 1940

### Narrative
A diesel railcar crossed Thrace along a flooded river plain full of cranes [Letter p. 17 — "diesel railcar"]. After Alexandroupolis came padded window seats through green Macedonian hills with snow peaks behind. There was no tobacco in sight, and none of the famous sun [Letter p. 17 — "the ride through Macedonia was beautiful"]. They reached Salonika at 10 PM and spent a cramped night in a full compartment.

### Features & Media
* **Nature Card:** Cranes and wetlands of the Evros (Maritsa) delta.

## Stop: Piraeus
**Coordinates:** 37.9475° N, 23.6372° E | **Date:** Jan 22, 1940, noon

### Narrative
A ship for Brindisi was leaving at 12. They rushed by an "antediluvian cab" and a motorboat paid in US dollars and reached the 3,500-ton *Egitto* "at the very last second" [Letter p. 20 — "at the very last second"].

## Leg: Piraeus → Corinth Canal → Brindisi (SS *Egitto*)
**Date:** Jan 22–23, 1940

### Narrative
In the late afternoon a small tug towed the ship through the roughly 3 km Corinth Canal, "a marvel of engineering" [Letter p. 20 — "towed by a small tugboat"]. Hope returned: the *Conte di Savoia* would sail on the 24th [Letter p. 20 — "The Conte di Savoia was set to sail on the 24th"].

## Stop: Brindisi
**Coordinates:** 40.6327° N, 17.9419° E | **Date:** Jan 23, 1940

### Narrative
Passport and baggage checks ran so long that the 1 PM Naples train left while the baggage clerk was at breakfast. Strauss was on board; Fritz was left behind with the trunks. "So there I sat, alone in Brindisi" [Letter p. 20 — "alone in Brindisi"].

## Leg: Brindisi → Foggia → Naples (bus and night train, alone)
**Date:** Jan 23–24, 1940

### Narrative
Fritz took a 3 PM bus to Foggia hoping to beat Strauss, who was on the southern rail route. The connection left only around midnight, and he reached Naples at 6:30 AM to find Strauss already waiting [Letter p. 20 — "landing in Naples at 6:30 in the morning"].

## Stop: Naples
**Coordinates:** 40.8518° N, 14.2681° E | **Date:** Jan 24, 1940, morning

### Narrative
Fritz had been looking forward to phoning home the whole trip, but was told Germany was not accepting calls from abroad. At 9 they watched the *Conte di Savoia* come in [Letter p. 20 — "watched the Conte di Savoia pull in"].
