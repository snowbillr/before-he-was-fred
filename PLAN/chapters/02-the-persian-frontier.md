# Chapter 2: The Persian Frontier
**Route:** Tabriz → Khoy → Maku → Bazargan → Gürbulak → Doğubayazıt → Kızıldize
**Dates:** Dec 21–23, 1939
**Mode of travel:** Crowded bus, ancient junk-laden bus, then a 1.5-ton Chevrolet truck

## Chapter Overview
In Tabriz a chance meeting with a Lithuanian-Jewish pharmacist secures a truck to Trabzon. Two bone-rattling bus rides follow across Iranian Azerbaijan, with tea in an opium den and flatbread eaten "like a giant cigarette". Then the Iranian and Turkish border posts under a fog-shrouded Mount Ararat, and the first snowbound night in Turkey.

---

## Stop: Tabriz (second day)
**Coordinates:** 38.0962° N, 46.2738° E | **Date:** Dec 21, 1939

### Narrative
The luggage arrived and Fritz finally changed out of shoes that had pinched him badly [Letter p. 2 — "changed my shoes"]. Talks with Hamid-Aga went nowhere until a sympathetic older pharmacist opposite translated via French. When Strauss mentioned they were Jewish emigrants, the man revealed he was a Lithuanian Jew, Isaac Rabinowitsch, and helped them "in a touching way" [Letter p. 2 — "a Lithuanian Jew"]. In the bazaar they booked seats on a 1.5-ton Chevrolet truck, with room for 3 passengers, to Trabzon for 130 Toman. They would go by bus as far as Bazargan while the firm's manager rode in the truck [Letter p. 2 — "1.5-ton Chevrolet with room for 3 persons"].

### Features & Media
* **Character Card:** Isaac Rabinowitsch, pharmacist of Tabriz.
* **Archival Photography:** The Tabriz bazaar (a UNESCO World Heritage Site; see `PLAN/media/chapter-02.md`).

## Leg: Tabriz → Khoy (night bus along the Julfa railway)
**Date:** Dec 21, 1939, ~5:15 PM → Dec 22, 1:00 AM

### Narrative
The 3 o'clock bus left after 5 PM, "stuffed full", with passengers packed even in the aisle [Letter p. 2 — "The bus was stuffed full"]. It drove into the night beside the railway line to Julfa, crossing it many times. At a supper stop in a small village they drank tea in vast quantities and watched the men praying. Their provisions had run out, so they ate sheep's cheese and sheets of flatbread "of the best quality" that Fritz called "hand towels" [Letter p. 2 — "of the best quality"]. The innkeeper asked 2 Kran for nine glasses of tea and mountains of bread and cheese, and was visibly moved when they paid 10 Shahi on top [Letter p. 3 — "10 Shahi on top of the 2 Kran"].

### Features & Media
* **Cultural Card:** *Lavash* and *sangak*: thin Iranian flatbreads, the likely "hand towels" (*Handtücher*).
* **Railway Spotter:** The Tabriz–Julfa railway (opened 1916), crossed repeatedly by the road.

## Stop: Khoy
**Coordinates:** 38.5524° N, 44.9521° E | **Date:** Dec 22, 1939, 1:00 AM to 3:00 PM

### Narrative
Arriving at 1 AM, they hauled their luggage through the streets and pounded on the door of the Esplanade hotel, with a policeman helping them make noise. Given the deluxe two-bed room, they "crept back together into one bed" [Letter p. 3 — "crept back together into one bed"]. In the bazaar they bought thick, colorful wool socks for 3 Kran a pair, "worn by almost all people there", and lunched on *dough* (sour yogurt drink) and kebab [Letter p. 3 — "colorful wool socks for 3 Kran"].

### Features & Media
* **Cultural Card:** Hand-knitted Azerbaijani/Kurdish wool socks (*jurab*) and *doogh*.

## Leg: Khoy → Maku (ancient bus over the mountains)
**Date:** Dec 22, 1939, 3:00 PM → 11:00 PM

### Narrative
An "ancient bus" piled high with junk carried just seven passengers. Uphill it was "slower than a pedestrian" and stopped every 2 km to pour water into the boiling radiator [Letter p. 3 — "every 2 km it was stopped"]. At dusk they stopped in a village and drank tea in "a picturesque opium den" [Letter p. 3 — "picturesque opium den"]. Ten kilometers before Maku their Chevrolet was waiting. The two drivers dragged them into a dive where they ate "hand-towels" rolled up "like a giant cigarette" around cheese, fat, or a flour-sugar-mutton-fat porridge [Letter p. 3 — "rolled it up like a giant cigarette"]. Fritz found the moonlit mountain drive "overall delightful", even though the roads were mostly "below the level of a German dirt road" [Letter p. 3 — "below the level of a German dirt"].

### Features & Media
* **Animated Route Segment:** The stuttering ascent (the bus stops every 2 km) and the moonlit night drive.
* **Interactive Recipe Card:** How to eat flatbread rolled around cheese, Maku-style.

## Stop: Maku
**Coordinates:** 39.2931° N, 44.4533° E | **Date:** Dec 22–23, 1939 (overnight)

### Narrative
Maku had no hotel, not even a teahouse. The two slept on benches pushed together in the bus waiting room [Letter p. 3 — "2 benches were placed next to each other"]. In the morning they saw the village of red-tinted houses clinging "like birds' nests" under a vertical rock wall that "threatened to crush everything below it" [Letter p. 3 — "like birds' nests"].

### Features & Media
* **Archival Photography:** Maku and its overhanging cliff (see `PLAN/media/chapter-02.md`).

## Leg: Maku → Bazargan (first ride in the Chevrolet)
**Date:** Dec 23, 1939, 9:00 AM

### Narrative
For the first time they rode in the Chevrolet. It had only three seats, so the chauffeur sat on the mudguard for the short drive [Letter p. 3 — "the chauffeur had to sit on the mudguard"].

## Stop: Bazargan (Iranian customs)
**Coordinates:** 39.3891° N, 44.3855° E | **Date:** Dec 23, 1939

### Narrative
The customs director, who suffered from "2 bad teeth", received them in good French. While his assistant searched the luggage, the director consulted the two dentists about his teeth, and they gave home-remedy advice [Letter p. 4 — "the director consulted us about his teeth"]. Strauss's photo album smoothed the inspection, and the director sold Fritz an Iraqi banknote (denomination unclear in the scan) for 10 Toman [Letter p. 4 — "which I acquired for 10 Toman"]. Mount Ararat stayed hidden in "black, impenetrable fog", so they could not look for Noah's Ark [Letter p. 4 — "black, impenetrable"]. They saw the wrecked Turkish airplanes awaiting pickup, sent a telegram to Fritz's parents, and ate a last real Iranian chicken lunch in Abdil's two-room mud hut [Letter p. 4 — "the telegram to you"].

### Features & Media
* **Visual Reveal:** A fog layer over a photograph of Mount Ararat. A mouseover clears the fog.
* **Primary Source Highlight:** Two dentists doing a consultation at a border desk.

## Stop: Gürbulak (Turkish customs)
**Coordinates:** 39.3950° N, 44.3800° E | **Date:** Dec 23, 1939

### Narrative
Nobody spoke Persian or any language they knew, so Ismael the chauffeur interpreted. Customs confiscated two new tires from the driver and searched the car "down to the smallest detail", while the two travelers wrote rude words in the fresh snow [Letter p. 4 — "They took 2 brand-new tires from the chauffeur"].

## Leg: Gürbulak → Doğubayazıt (Bayazit) → Kızıldize (into the snow)
**Date:** Dec 23, 1939, after dark

### Narrative
In Bayazit (today Doğubayazıt), about 20 km from the border, they skipped the menu, walked into the kitchen and pointed at the twenty pots on the hearth [Letter p. 4 — "we pointed at what we wanted"]. Snow began to fall and thickened as they drove 25 km more to the snowed-in village of Kızıldize. It had only a miserable inn with a side room of ten cots [Letter p. 4 — "10 plank beds"].

### Features & Media
* **Context Card:** Bayazit, later renamed Doğubayazıt when the town moved down from the İshak Paşa Palace.
