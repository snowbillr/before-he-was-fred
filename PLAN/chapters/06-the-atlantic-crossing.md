# Chapter 6: The Atlantic Crossing
**Route:** Naples → (SS *Conte di Savoia*) → Gibraltar → Atlantic Ocean → New York City
**Dates:** Jan 24 – ~Jan 31, 1940 (the letter itself is dated Brooklyn, Feb 21, 1940)
**Mode of travel:** Italian Line ocean liner

## Chapter Overview
After two months of overland hardship the last leg is almost anticlimactic: a poor outer cabin, emigrant passengers, Skat with Hungarians, a British contraband stop at Gibraltar, seasickness, and finally Manhattan rising out of the haze.

> **Arrival date:** The letter does not name the day. The Brooklyn section, dated 21.2.40, says they "reached our goal safe and sound 3 weeks ago" [Letter p. 15 — "3 weeks ago"](../letter/pages/page-15.json#p15-s10). With a Jan 24 departure from Naples and Gibraltar on Jan 26, that puts arrival at about Jan 31, 1940. Confirm the exact date against the Ellis Island / NARA passenger manifest for the *Conte di Savoia*.

## Leg: Naples → Gibraltar (SS *Conte di Savoia*)
**Date:** Jan 24–26, 1940

### Narrative
"Exactly at noon, it sailed away with us and our belongings." [Letter p. 20 — "At noon sharp"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s24). Their "terribly poor outer cabin" boomed as the water slammed against it. Most passengers were emigrants from Germany [Letter p. 20 — "emigrants coming from Germany"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s27). They shared a dining table with some Hungarians and "soon put together our long-awaited game of Skat" [Letter p. 20 — "the long-awaited Skat game"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s29).

### Features & Media
* **Ship Card:** SS *Conte di Savoia* (Italian Line, 1932): Blue Riband contender, gyroscopic stabilizers, and its 1940 neutral-flag crossings (see `PLAN/media/chapter-06.md`).
* **Context Card:** Jewish emigration through Italian ports in 1939–1940.

## Stop: Gibraltar (British contraband control)
**Coordinates:** 36.1408° N, 5.3536° W | **Date:** Jan 26, 1940

### Narrative
Gibraltar lay "so peacefully and harmless-looking", with southern Spain on one side and "the blue mountains of Africa" on the other. The ship anchored for six hours while British officials came aboard [Letter p. 20 — "there for 6 hours"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s32).

### Features & Media
* **Historical Card:** The British blockade's contraband control at Gibraltar in the Phoney War. Neutral ships were stopped and searched.

## Leg: The Atlantic
**Date:** ~Jan 27–31, 1940

### Narrative
Fritz spent most of the crossing in the cabin "right on the verge of seasickness", then recovered the day before arrival and ate "my way up and down the menus" [Letter p. 20 — "up and down the menus"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s36).

### Features & Media
* **Archival Media:** *Conte di Savoia* menus, deck plans and ocean views.

## Stop: New York City
**Coordinates:** 40.7128° N, 74.0060° W | **Date:** ~Jan 31, 1940

### Narrative
"The skyscrapers of Manhattan rose out of the haze, and we glided past the huge bridges… a truly impressive end to our remarkable journey!" [Letter p. 20 — "skyscrapers of Manhattan rose out of the haze"](../letter/pages/page-20.json#p20-s38). The letter does not mention the Statue of Liberty, so the site should not claim it.

### Features & Media
* **Visual Reveal:** Lower Manhattan skyline, c. 1940, fading in from haze.
* **Epilogue:** The letter's Brooklyn dateline (Feb 21, 1940), and the transition from Fritz to "Fred".
